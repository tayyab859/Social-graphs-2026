The question

Does a new representation change your neighbors?

TF-IDF rewards distinctive shared words. Word2Vec learns which words occur in similar surroundings. We compared TF-IDF document vectors with TF-IDF-weighted averages of word vectors trained on the same Marvel corpus.

The result is a disagreement, not a winner: only 2.23 of five neighbors overlap on average. Wikipedia links provide a third view, not ground-truth labels for semantic similarity.

Read the pages

Four checks behind the rankings

These illustrative cases were selected after viewing the results, not as a random validation sample. An AI-assisted inspection of the frozen source passages supports the interpretations below. Shared-word lists help inspect TF-IDF; they are not an attribution method for the dense model.

Text check · Storm

TF-IDF → Human Torch; embedding → Wolverine

The shared surname “Storm” drives the count-based match. The frozen text describes Storm and Wolverine as X-Men teammates and as having a relationship. The embedding match is plausible here, but it does not prove the model understands either character.

Text check · Wolverine

TF-IDF → Ultimate Wolverine; embedding → Deadpool

The first match captures another version of the same character. The second connects two pages that discuss healing factors. These are different, defensible meanings of similarity. Neither is automatically the correct answer.

Text check · Black Widow

TF-IDF → Yelena Belova; embedding → Sabra

Yelena shares the Black Widow identity and Red Room training. The Sabra page describes a film version as a former Black Widow assassin. This is a cautionary case: whole-article vectors mix comic continuity, adaptations and publication history.

Text check · Spider-Man

Both methods → Spider-Man Noir

Both pages identify a Peter Parker / Spider-Man identity. Agreement is unsurprising here and does not establish that either method has recovered deeper meaning.

Sensitivity check

How stable are the matches?

Changing only the Word2Vec seed from 0 to 1 keeps 4.32 of five neighbors on average. Changing the maximum context window from 5 to 10, with seed 0, keeps 3.97 of five.

This is a limited sensitivity check, not a confidence interval. The two text methods differ more than these particular training changes, but individual neighbors still move.

Reproduce the experiment

Small enough to inspect.

  1. Use the official frozen text archive and Week 1 node/edge files. Match by node ID, not display label; all 303 IDs must agree.
  2. Lowercase; tokenize ASCII letter sequences of length at least two. TF-IDF uses scikit-learn English stopwords, min_df=2, max_df=0.8, smoothed IDF and L2 normalization. Names remain. This differs from the course’s hand-calculation formula.
  3. Train Skip-gram on sentence-split full text: 100 dimensions, maximum window 5, min_count=5, 5 negatives, 20 epochs, seed 0, one worker. Word2Vec sees stopwords during training. Default dynamic windows and frequent-word subsampling remain enabled.
  4. Multiply each word vector by its document’s TF-IDF weight and sum; cosine normalizes the resulting vector. Division by the sum of weights would give the same cosine. Unavailable word vectors contribute zero; covered terms account for 92.4% of total TF-IDF weight.
  5. Rank by cosine, exclude self-matches, and compare the top five. Collapse links to an undirected graph only for the linked-neighbor comparison.

What this does not establish

A match is a lead to investigate.

Our small, domain-specific model is not a general semantic oracle. Static vectors merge word senses; weighted averaging loses word order; names remain in both models. Whole articles combine biography, media adaptations and editorial boilerplate. The ASCII tokenizer also loses accented characters. High dense-vector cosines can reflect common directions in the embedding space.

We did not perform a blinded human relevance study. Four selected case checks cannot establish overall accuracy. The next improvement would be a fixed, independently labeled sample of matches and a comparison of biography-only text with complete pages.

What surprised us: the Sabra match looks plausible until its source is inspected: the connection is partly an adaptation. Changing the representation changes which parts of a page become prominent. It does not remove the need to read.

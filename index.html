Research question

Does attention travel both ways?

In the course network, an edge A → B means page A links to page B. We asked how often the reverse edge also exists, which characters attract the most one-way attention, and which characters send many links that are not returned.

This keeps direction visible. Turning the network undirected would make a mutual relationship and a one-way mention look identical.

What the direction reveals

Three findings

01

Most links do not come back

Only 700 of 1,784 directed links belong to mutual pairs. Network reciprocity is therefore 0.392: roughly two in five arrows have a reverse arrow.

02

Spider-Man is an attention magnet

Spider-Man receives 106 links and sends 9. All 9 outgoing links are mutual, leaving 97 incoming-only links. High visibility does not imply balanced linking.

03

Wolverine has the most mutual neighbours

Wolverine exchanges links with 14 characters, the largest mutual-neighbour count. But 46 additional pages link to Wolverine without receiving a link back.

Interpretation

Links record editing, not social relationships

A mutual link means two Wikipedia articles mention one another in their running text. It does not prove that the fictional characters are friends, enemies, or even directly interact.

Incoming-only links can indicate broad recognition: many editors need to position their character relative to a famous figure. Outgoing-only links can reflect a page that explains a character through many relationships. These are interpretations of editorial structure, not measurements of personality.
